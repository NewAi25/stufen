# 14. What each CEFR level means in real life

Version 0.1. Research note. Written 2026-09-09 from the sources listed at the end. Nothing here is a legal or pedagogical opinion of the author; where a figure could not be checked against a primary source it is marked "not verified".

## 1. Why this document exists

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) is the Council of Europe's scale for describing what a language user can do. It has six levels, A1 and A2 (basic user), B1 and B2 (independent user), C1 and C2 (proficient user). Each level is defined by can do statements about listening, reading, speaking and writing in real situations. It is not defined by a grammar list, a word count or a number of hours. Grammar and vocabulary are means; the level is the outcome. German law adopts the same scale word for word: § 2 of the Residence Act (AufenthG) defines "einfache" German as A1, "hinreichende" as A2, "ausreichende" as B1, "gute" as B2 and "beherrscht" as C1 (S1).

Stufen reports an estimated level. That estimate is only honest if "you have reached B2" in the app means the learner can do what the Council of Europe's global scale says a B2 user can do, in the situations a person meets when they move to Germany. This document sets out what the levels mean, what Germany requires each level for, which exams certify them, how long they take, and what that implies for the curriculum spine in `docs/05-curriculum-spine.md`.

Two sources anchor the level descriptions. The global scale (Council of Europe, CEFR Table 1) gives one paragraph per level (S2). The self assessment grid (CEFR Table 2, reproduced on the EU's Europass site) gives one cell per level per skill (S3). Both are paraphrased closely below. The Goethe Institut's own level descriptions mirror the global scale almost word for word (S4). The coe.int pages refused automated access on the day of writing, so the Table 1 wording was checked against the Wikipedia reproduction and the Goethe page; the coe.int URL is still the reference.

## 2. The levels

### A1

**Global scale (S2).** Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases aimed at concrete needs. Can introduce self and others, ask and answer questions about personal details such as where they live, people they know and things they have. Can interact in a simple way if the other person talks slowly and clearly and is prepared to help.

**Self assessment grid (S3).**

| Skill | A1 |
|-------|----|
| Listening | Recognises familiar words and very basic phrases about self, family and immediate surroundings when people speak slowly and clearly |
| Reading | Understands familiar names, words and very simple sentences, for example on notices, posters and in catalogues |
| Spoken interaction | Interacts simply if the other person repeats or rephrases slowly and helps formulate what the learner is trying to say; asks and answers simple questions on immediate needs or very familiar topics |
| Spoken production | Uses simple phrases and sentences to describe where they live and people they know |
| Writing | Writes a short simple postcard; fills in forms with personal details such as name, nationality and address |

**In real life this means**

| Area | A1 can |
|------|--------|
| At work | Greet colleagues, say their name, job and where they are from; understand "Pause", "Feierabend", "morgen um neun"; answer yes or no questions asked slowly |
| Authorities and offices | Fill in the personal details on the Anmeldung form (name, date of birth, nationality, address); say "Ich habe einen Termin um zehn Uhr"; hand over a passport when asked |
| Housing and everyday services | Read a shop sign, opening hours and prices; buy a ticket at a machine; say "Ich möchte ein Brot, bitte"; give their address to a delivery driver |
| Health | Say "Ich habe Schmerzen hier" and point; give name and insurance card at reception; understand "Nehmen Sie Platz" |
| Social life | Say hello, introduce themselves, say what they like, ask "Woher kommst du?"; understand a slow, patient neighbour |
| Media and study | Read a short text message, a menu, a timetable, a name badge; follow a classroom instruction given slowly with gestures |

**Still hard at this level**

| Area | A1 cannot yet |
|------|---------------|
| At work | Follow any real meeting, phone call or instruction spoken at normal speed; write a work email |
| Authorities and offices | Understand a letter from the Ausländerbehörde or Finanzamt; explain a problem with an application |
| Housing and everyday services | Read a rental contract; report a fault to a landlord beyond "kaputt"; deal with a phone hotline |
| Health | Describe symptoms with any detail; understand a diagnosis or dosage instructions |
| Social life | Hold a conversation that goes beyond a fixed exchange; understand jokes, dialect or two people talking to each other |
| Media and study | Read a newspaper article or a website beyond isolated words; follow the news, a film or a podcast |

### A2

**Global scale (S2).** Can understand sentences and frequently used expressions about areas of most immediate relevance (basic personal and family information, shopping, local geography, employment). Can communicate in simple routine tasks that need a simple direct exchange of information on familiar matters. Can describe in simple terms their background, immediate environment and matters of immediate need.

**Self assessment grid (S3).**

| Skill | A2 |
|-------|----|
| Listening | Understands phrases and the most frequent vocabulary on matters of immediate personal relevance (family, shopping, local area, employment); catches the main point of short, clear, simple messages and announcements |
| Reading | Reads very short simple texts; finds specific predictable information in adverts, prospectuses, menus and timetables; understands short simple personal letters |
| Spoken interaction | Communicates in simple routine tasks that need a direct exchange of information on familiar topics; handles very short social exchanges but cannot usually keep the conversation going |
| Spoken production | Uses a series of phrases and sentences to describe family, other people, living conditions, education and present or last job in simple terms |
| Writing | Writes short simple notes and messages on matters of immediate need; writes a very simple personal letter, for example to thank someone |

**In real life this means**

| Area | A2 can |
|------|--------|
| At work | Describe their previous job and training in simple sentences; understand a simple instruction and ask "Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?"; write a short message "Ich bin heute krank, ich komme morgen" |
| Authorities and offices | Book an appointment at a counter or by simple online form; answer routine questions at the Bürgeramt (address, marital status, employer); understand a short standard letter with an appointment date if it is simply worded |
| Housing and everyday services | Ask about a flat, its price and when it is free; tell the landlord "Die Heizung ist kaputt" and ask for a repair date in simple words; open a bank account with a helpful clerk; return an item to a shop |
| Health | Make a doctor's appointment by phone with a patient receptionist; describe common symptoms (fever, cough, pain since two days); understand simple instructions such as "dreimal am Tag nach dem Essen" |
| Social life | Small talk about weather, weekend, food, family; invite someone and accept or decline; understand a neighbour who speaks clearly |
| Media and study | Read short simple news items for learners, adverts, public notices, a short email; follow slow, clear learner audio; use a simple app or website in German |

**Still hard at this level**

| Area | A2 cannot yet |
|------|---------------|
| At work | Take part in a team meeting; understand a colleague explaining a process at normal speed; write a structured email to HR |
| Authorities and offices | Read and act on a multi page official letter; argue a decision; understand legal or tax vocabulary |
| Housing and everyday services | Understand the clauses of a rental contract or utility bill; negotiate with a landlord about a deposit; handle a customer service call where the agent speaks fast |
| Health | Explain a medical history; understand a doctor's explanation of a diagnosis or a consent form |
| Social life | Keep a conversation going on a new topic; follow a group of native speakers talking among themselves; understand irony |
| Media and study | Read an ordinary newspaper article; follow the evening news, a film or a lecture |

Note. The German Test for Immigrants (DTZ) sits across A2 and B1 and is the end test of the state integration course (S5). A learner who leaves the course with A2 gets a certificate but not the "Zertifikat Integrationskurs", which needs B1.

### B1

**Global scale (S2).** Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters met regularly at work, school and leisure. Can deal with most situations likely to arise while travelling where the language is spoken. Can produce simple connected text on familiar topics or of personal interest. Can describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes and ambitions and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.

**Self assessment grid (S3).**

| Skill | B1 |
|-------|----|
| Listening | Understands the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters at work, school and leisure; understands the main point of many radio or TV programmes on current affairs or topics of interest when the delivery is relatively slow and clear |
| Reading | Understands texts in high frequency everyday or job related language; understands descriptions of events, feelings and wishes in personal letters |
| Spoken interaction | Deals with most situations that arise while travelling; enters unprepared into conversation on familiar topics of personal interest or daily life (family, hobbies, work, travel, current events) |
| Spoken production | Connects phrases simply to describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes and ambitions; briefly gives reasons and explanations for opinions and plans; narrates a story or the plot of a book or film |
| Writing | Writes simple connected text on familiar topics or of personal interest; writes personal letters describing experiences and impressions |

**In real life this means**

| Area | B1 can |
|------|--------|
| At work | Follow a clearly run team meeting on familiar topics and contribute a point; explain a task to a colleague; ask for clarification; write a simple but complete email (request, apology, update); handle a routine phone call; explain why something is a problem (S4) |
| Authorities and offices | Deal with the Bürgeramt, Ausländerbehörde and Jobcenter counter unaided on standard matters; understand most standard letters and know when to ask for help; fill in longer forms; give a coherent account of their situation |
| Housing and everyday services | Explain to a landlord that the heating is broken, when it stopped, and ask when it will be fixed; understand the main points of a rental contract with a dictionary; make a complaint in a shop; change a phone or electricity contract; write a short formal letter to cancel something |
| Health | Describe symptoms, history and what has been tried; understand the main points of a diagnosis and treatment plan; ask a pharmacist how to take a medicine; deal with a sick note and inform the employer |
| Social life | Join a conversation among friendly people on everyday topics; tell a story about their weekend or a trip; state and briefly justify an opinion; understand most of a clearly spoken conversation partner |
| Media and study | Read simplified news, straightforward newspaper articles on familiar topics, blog posts, instructions; follow slow clear radio or TV items; follow a training course delivered in clear German with visual support |

**Still hard at this level**

| Area | B1 cannot yet |
|------|---------------|
| At work | Follow a fast meeting between native speakers about a technical topic; lead a discussion; write a report or a formal complaint that reads professionally; argue a position under pressure |
| Authorities and offices | Read a legal notice, a court letter or a tax assessment in full; object to a decision in writing with a proper argument |
| Housing and everyday services | Understand the fine print of contracts, house rules and insurance policies; negotiate a rent reduction with reasons and counter arguments |
| Health | Understand a specialist's detailed explanation or a consent form for surgery; discuss options and risks |
| Social life | Follow a lively group conversation, dialect, fast speech, humour and cultural references; talk in depth about abstract topics |
| Media and study | Read a broadsheet editorial, a novel or a specialist text; follow a university lecture, a fast podcast or a film without subtitles |

### B2

**Global scale (S2).** Can understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in their own field. Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers possible without strain for either side. Can produce clear detailed text on a wide range of subjects and explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options.

**Self assessment grid (S3).**

| Skill | B2 |
|-------|----|
| Listening | Understands extended speech and lectures and follows complex lines of argument if the topic is reasonably familiar; understands most TV news and current affairs programmes and most films in standard dialect |
| Reading | Reads articles and reports on contemporary problems in which writers adopt particular attitudes or viewpoints; understands contemporary literary prose |
| Spoken interaction | Interacts with enough fluency and spontaneity for regular interaction with native speakers; takes an active part in discussion in familiar contexts, accounting for and sustaining views |
| Spoken production | Presents clear detailed descriptions on a wide range of subjects in their field of interest; explains a viewpoint on a topical issue giving advantages and disadvantages |
| Writing | Writes clear detailed text on a wide range of subjects; writes an essay or report passing on information or giving reasons for or against a point of view; writes letters highlighting the personal significance of events |

**In real life this means**

| Area | B2 can |
|------|--------|
| At work | Do a job interview in German; take part actively in a normal team meeting, including disagreeing with reasons; present their own work; write professional emails, meeting notes and short reports; discuss a technical topic in their own field with colleagues; ask for a raise with a prepared argument |
| Authorities and offices | Handle almost any standard administrative process alone, including reading a multi page decision and writing a reasoned objection; explain a complex personal situation to a caseworker; understand what a lawyer or adviser says in plain terms |
| Housing and everyday services | Read and question a rental contract and Nebenkostenabrechnung; negotiate with a landlord or agent; write a formal complaint; deal with insurance claims and customer service escalations; understand house rules and Eigentümerversammlung notices |
| Health | Discuss diagnosis, treatment options and risks with a doctor; understand a consent form with some effort; act as a first line interpreter for a family member in routine matters; nursing recognition in Germany is set at this level (S9) |
| Social life | Follow and join most group conversations among native speakers on general topics; hold a long conversation with a new acquaintance; understand most humour that does not rely on dialect or deep cultural reference; watch a film or series in standard German |
| Media and study | Read newspapers and non fiction; follow the news, documentaries and most podcasts; follow a lecture or training in their own field; many German universities set B2 as the minimum for some programmes (S13) |

**Still hard at this level**

| Area | B2 cannot yet |
|------|---------------|
| At work | Follow fast, overlapping, informal or dialect heavy talk in a large meeting; write a polished formal document (contract text, official report, grant application) without revision by a native speaker; catch every nuance of tone in negotiation |
| Authorities and offices | Read primary legal text (laws, court judgements) fluently; argue a point in the register expected in formal legal correspondence |
| Housing and everyday services | Understand every clause of dense contract or insurance German at first reading |
| Health | Doctors need C1 level medical German for licensing in addition to B2 general German (S8); a B2 speaker still misses implicit meaning and specialist terms in medical talk |
| Social life | Follow a fast group of friends using slang, dialect and shared references; pick up irony and understatement reliably; adjust register finely between friends, colleagues and officials |
| Media and study | Read demanding literature, academic articles or satirical writing with ease; follow a fast, unstructured lecture; university admission for German taught degrees generally needs TestDaF 4 or DSH 2, above B2 (S12) |

### C1

**Global scale (S2).** Can understand a wide range of demanding longer texts and recognise implicit meaning. Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions. Can use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic and professional purposes. Can produce clear, well structured, detailed text on complex subjects, showing controlled use of organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices.

**Self assessment grid (S3).**

| Skill | C1 |
|-------|----|
| Listening | Understands extended speech even when it is not clearly structured and when relationships are only implied; understands TV programmes and films without too much effort |
| Reading | Understands long complex factual and literary texts, appreciating distinctions of style; understands specialised articles and longer technical instructions even outside their own field |
| Spoken interaction | Expresses self fluently and spontaneously without much searching for expressions; uses language flexibly and effectively for social and professional purposes; formulates ideas and opinions precisely and relates their contribution skilfully to those of others |
| Spoken production | Presents clear detailed descriptions of complex subjects, integrating sub themes, developing points and rounding off with an appropriate conclusion |
| Writing | Expresses self in clear well structured text, giving points of view at some length; writes about complex subjects in a letter, essay or report, underlining what is salient; chooses a style appropriate to the reader |

**In real life this means**

| Area | C1 can |
|------|--------|
| At work | Lead meetings and negotiations; present to management and clients; write reports, proposals, policy text and formal correspondence that need only light editing; handle conflict, feedback and performance talks in German; work in a German speaking professional environment at full capacity |
| Authorities and offices | Read laws, decisions and legal letters and understand what they imply; write formal objections and applications in the expected register; deal with courts, notaries and tax advisers without an interpreter |
| Housing and everyday services | Buy property, read a notary contract with the notary's explanation, chair a flat owners' meeting, argue a dispute with a management company in writing |
| Health | Doctors: this is the level of professional medical German the Fachsprachprüfung tests (S8); patients: understand a specialist fully, read medical reports, discuss subtle options |
| Social life | Take part in any conversation among native speakers, including fast informal talk; understand most humour, irony and cultural references; shift register naturally between friends, colleagues and officials |
| Media and study | Read broadsheets, academic texts and most literature; follow lectures, debates and satire; study in German at university, which is the level TestDaF 4 and DSH 2 sit at (S12) |

**Still hard at this level**

| Area | C1 cannot yet |
|------|---------------|
| At work | Write with the stylistic precision of an educated native speaker in every genre without any review; always catch the finest shades of tone |
| Authorities and offices | Draft legal text to a professional standard |
| Housing and everyday services | Little that matters; occasional rare vocabulary in dense contract or technical German |
| Health | Very specialised or dialect coloured patient speech can still cause slips |
| Social life | Strong regional dialect, very fast group banter, very old or very young speakers, literary and historical allusions |
| Media and study | Highly stylised literature, legal commentary, dense philosophy; consistently choosing the one idiom a native speaker would choose |

### C2

**Global scale (S2).** Can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read. Can summarise information from different spoken and written sources, reconstructing arguments and accounts in a coherent presentation. Can express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in the most complex situations.

**Self assessment grid (S3).**

| Skill | C2 |
|-------|----|
| Listening | No difficulty understanding any spoken language, live or broadcast, even at fast native speed, given some time to get used to the accent |
| Reading | Reads with ease virtually all written forms, including abstract, structurally or linguistically complex texts such as manuals, specialised articles and literary works |
| Spoken interaction | Takes part effortlessly in any conversation or discussion; good familiarity with idiom and colloquialism; conveys finer shades of meaning; can backtrack and restructure around a difficulty so smoothly that others hardly notice |
| Spoken production | Presents a clear, smoothly flowing description or argument in a style appropriate to the context, with an effective logical structure that helps the listener notice and remember the key points |
| Writing | Writes clear, smoothly flowing text in an appropriate style; writes complex letters, reports or articles that present a case with an effective logical structure; writes summaries and reviews of professional or literary works |

**In real life this means**

| Area | C2 can |
|------|--------|
| At work | Work as a writer, lawyer, teacher, journalist, translator or senior manager in German; draft and edit text for publication; mediate between parties and registers; summarise a long technical debate for management in a few sentences |
| Authorities and offices | Everything at C1 plus reading historical or archaic legal language and writing to a professional standard |
| Housing and everyday services | Nothing in this area remains hard |
| Health | Everything at C1; understands dialect coloured patient speech and specialist jargon |
| Social life | Indistinguishable from an educated native speaker in most settings; understands regional variation, wordplay and literary allusion; the Goethe C2 exam includes writing about a set literary work (S4) |
| Media and study | Reads and summarises literature, law, philosophy and science across sources; the Goethe C2 GDS exempts the holder from any university language test (S12) |

**Still hard at this level**

| Area | C2 cannot yet |
|------|---------------|
| All areas | Only what is hard for a native speaker too: a strong unfamiliar dialect, very specialised jargon outside their own field, highly stylised literary or legal prose at first reading. Occasional article or gender slips on rare nouns remain typical of very advanced learners |

## 3. Which level is required for what in Germany

The Residence Act ties the everyday German words for language ability to CEFR levels (S1). Many visas for work carry no legal language requirement at all; the demand comes from employers, recognition bodies and universities. The Make it in Germany chart of language requirements by visa type (Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs, as of January 2026) is the source for the visa rows (S6).

| Purpose | Level | Source and notes |
|---------|-------|------------------|
| Spouse joining a partner in Germany (family reunion visa) | A1 ("einfache Deutschkenntnisse") | Certificate from an ALTE certified provider such as Goethe, telc, ÖSD, shown before entry. Exemptions: evident ability at the interview, disability, spouses of highly qualified workers, researchers, Blue Card holders and recognised refugees under conditions, low integration need such as university graduates, citizens of Australia, Israel, Japan, Canada, South Korea, New Zealand and the USA (S7, Federal Foreign Office leaflet) |
| Integration course target | B1, tested by the DTZ | General course 600 lesson units of language plus 100 of orientation; special courses up to 900; fast track 400. The "Zertifikat Integrationskurs" needs an overall DTZ result of B1 plus the "Leben in Deutschland" test. Cost 2.29 euro per 45 minute unit, so a 700 unit course is 1603 euro; free for people on benefits (S5) |
| Settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) | B1 ("ausreichende Kenntnisse") | § 9 Abs. 2 Nr. 7 AufenthG, normally after five years of residence, plus basic knowledge of the legal and social order (Nr. 8). Waivers for illness, disability and hardship (S1, S1b). EU Blue Card holders can apply after 27 months, or 21 months with B1; reported on the Make it in Germany EU Blue Card page, which blocked automated access on the day, so treat as reported not verified |
| Naturalisation | B1 | § 10 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 and Abs. 4 StAG: "Anforderungen einer Sprachprüfung der Stufe B 1". Accepted: Goethe B1, telc B1, ÖSD B1, DTZ with B1, integration course certificate, or German schooling or vocational training. Five years of residence since the June 2024 reform. Secondary sources report the three year fast track was removed again in October 2025; that changes residence years, not the language level (S1c, S10) |
| EU Blue Card | No legal requirement | S6, January 2026. Employers commonly ask for B2 in German speaking teams; that is practice, not law, and is not verified against any statistic |
| Work visa for qualified professionals, experienced workers, self employment, research | No legal requirement | S6 |
| Opportunity card (Chancenkarte) | German A1 or English B2 | S6 |
| Visa for recognition of a foreign qualification or recognition partnership | A2 | S6 |
| Vocational training (Ausbildung) visa and visa to look for a training place | B1 | S6. The chart adds that other requirements may apply in the recognition procedure. Schools and employers often prefer B2; that preference is not a legal rule and is not verified |
| Most skilled jobs | B2 in practice | Not a legal requirement. The Goethe Institut markets B2 as "live and work in Germany" and C1 as the level for effective professional use (S4). Treat "B2 for most jobs" as market practice, not law |
| Doctors and dentists (Approbation) | B2 general plus C1 medical German, tested by the Fachsprachprüfung | Conference of Health Ministers decision of 26 and 27 June 2014. Most states run the Fachsprachprüfung through the Landesärztekammer; a few accept C1 medical exams from other providers such as telc Deutsch B2 C1 Medizin. Format: three parts of 20 minutes each, simulated patient history, written doctor's letter, doctor to doctor talk; all three must pass. Berlin charges 550 euro per attempt, unlimited retakes. Marburger Bund overview last updated February 2026 (S8, S8b) |
| Nurses (Pflegefachperson) | B2 job related German | Recognition finder of the federal portal anerkennung-in-deutschland.de: proof of "berufsbezogene Deutschkenntnisse" at B2, by a certificate or the Fachsprachenprüfung Pflege; the certificate can be handed in after the application; waived if the training was in German. Implementation varies by state (S9) |
| University admission to German taught degrees | DSH 2 or TestDaF TDN 4 in all four parts, roughly C1 | Framework regulation RO-DT of HRK (23 July 2020) and KMK (28 November 2019), § 3 Abs. 5 and § 4 Abs. 5. Exempt under § 8: Goethe Zertifikat C2 GDS, ÖSD C2, telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule, DSD II, and a German language school leaving certificate. Universities may set lower entry (DSH 1) for specific programmes with an obligation to continue language courses. DAAD states most institutions require at least B2 and that TestDaF is offered in about 100 countries while DSH is only taken in Germany (S12, S13, S14) |
| Visa to look for a study place | Generally B2, depending on programme | S6 |

## 5. How long each level takes

The Goethe Institut publishes lesson unit ranges on its exam pages. One Unterrichtseinheit is 45 minutes. The ranges are cumulative from zero and the pages say they depend on prior knowledge and learning conditions (S4b to S4f). Cambridge English publishes cumulative guided learning hours per CEFR level for English and says about 200 guided hours are needed per level step, with the warning that intensity, age, prior languages and exposure outside lessons change the figure (S18). The Council of Europe itself does not publish hours per level; a coe.int figure could not be found (not verified). LanguageCert gives smaller per step figures for its own English exams (S19).

| Level | Goethe, lesson units of 45 min, cumulative | Goethe converted to clock hours | Cambridge, guided learning hours, cumulative | LanguageCert, hours per step |
|-------|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| A1 | 80 to 200 | 60 to 150 | 90 to 100 | 95 |
| A2 | 200 to 350 | 150 to 260 | 180 to 200 | 95 |
| B1 | 350 to 650 | 260 to 490 | 350 to 400 | 180 |
| B2 | 600 to 800 | 450 to 600 | 500 to 600 | 180 |
| C1 | 800 to 1000 | 600 to 750 | 700 to 800 | 200 |
| C2 | at least 1000 | at least 750 | 1000 to 1200 | 250 |

Two notes. First, these are guided hours, meaning time with a teacher or in a structured course. Homework and exposure come on top. Self study without a teacher is usually slower per hour because there is no correction and no forced production; the Cambridge page lists exposure and intensity as the variables that move the figure (S18). Stufen's tutor and grading loop is the substitute for the teacher, which is the whole bet of the product. Second, German is known to take longer than English for most learners because of case, gender and word order, but no primary source giving a German specific hour figure beyond Goethe's was found (not verified).

For the relocating professional in `docs/01-prd.md`, B2 in a year means 450 to 600 guided clock hours in twelve months, about nine to twelve hours a week including production practice. That is possible but it is a serious commitment, not a daily fifteen minute streak.

## 6. What this means for Stufen

The app's level estimate must be judged against the global scale in section 2, not against exercise accuracy. Rules for the curriculum spine and assessment boxes:

1. **A level checkpoint must contain real production.** Every checkpoint from A1 upward includes at least one unscripted spoken roleplay and one written task drawn from a real scenario for that level (for example B1: report a broken heating to a landlord and ask for a repair date; write the follow up email). The learner cannot pass the checkpoint on receptive items alone.

2. **Exercise accuracy can never raise a level on its own.** Mastery of grammar and vocabulary descriptors (`GRA`, `VOC`) is scaffolding evidence. A level is granted only when the production descriptors (`SPI`, `SPP`, `WRI`) for that level have graded samples at or above the threshold, and the checkpoint production tasks are passed. This tightens the rule in `docs/05` where 80 percent of all descriptors plus a 70 percent checkpoint could in theory be reached mostly through receptive and scaffolding items.

3. **The level map shows the official descriptor next to the evidence.** Each level card displays the global scale paragraph and the five grid cells from section 2 verbatim, with the source, beside the learner's evidence. The learner should be able to read "B2: can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers possible" and see the roleplay recordings that back it.

4. **The scenario library per level covers all six life areas from A1.** Work, authorities and offices, housing and everyday services, health, social life, media and study. `docs/01` starts the workplace and life track at B1. The grid shows real tasks at A1 (fill in the Anmeldung form) and A2 (write "Die Heizung ist kaputt" to the landlord, book a doctor's appointment), and the DTZ tests exactly those at A2 to B1. Start the track at A1 with simpler scenarios rather than at B1.

5. **Checkpoint tasks mirror the exam that certifies the level.** A1 to C2 follow the Goethe module shapes in section 4 (for example B2 writing: a forum post giving an opinion plus a formal message; B2 speaking: a short presentation plus a discussion in a pair). B1 also offers the DTZ shape, since that is the test most immigrants actually sit. C1 adds a TestDaF or telc C1 Hochschule style task for the study bound learner. Exam formats stay as data in `lang-de`.

6. **The "still hard" lists become negative checks.** For each level the tutor may deliberately include one task from the next level's "can" column in a checkpoint, ungraded, to calibrate. If a learner passes those easily the placement is too low; if they fail everything in their own level's column the level was granted too early. Log the result as calibration data, never as mastery.

7. **The app states what the level is good for.** When a level is reached, the level card shows the section 3 rows for that level (for example B1: settlement permit, naturalisation, integration course certificate, Ausbildung visa) with sources and dates, and says plainly that the app's estimate is not a certificate and names the exams that are.

8. **Time expectations are shown honestly.** The path length per level in `docs/05` should be checked against the Goethe unit ranges. A 90 lesson B1 path at 20 minutes a lesson is about 30 hours of app time against 260 to 490 guided hours in a classroom. Either the lessons are much denser than a classroom hour, which must be demonstrated by checkpoint results, or the path lengths and the daily loop need to be reconsidered. The app should show the learner the expected hours, not only the lesson count.

Points where the existing docs and the sources differ:

- `docs/05` exam alignment table says telc has no C2 exam. telc Deutsch C2 exists (S16). Update the row.
- `docs/05` calls the Goethe figures "guided hours". They are Unterrichtseinheiten of 45 minutes. The figures themselves match the Goethe pages. Label them as units or convert to clock hours.
- `docs/01` says the target is the level a workplace or visa office demands. For most work visas the law demands nothing (S6). The real demands are B1 for settlement and citizenship, B2 for regulated health professions and most employers, C1 for university and for doctors' professional language. The PRD should say this precisely.
- `docs/01` starts the life and work track at B1. The CEFR grid and the DTZ put office, housing and health tasks at A1 and A2 already. See rule 4.
